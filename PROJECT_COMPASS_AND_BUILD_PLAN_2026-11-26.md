## 3. READY MAKER ONE — the game determines the movie

The owner-canon objective is fully recorded in **[PROJECT_OBJECTIVE.md](PROJECT_OBJECTIVE.md)**. The central principle is:

> The game determines who wins. The history reveals how. The studio discovers and makes the movie inside it.

The long-horizon target is an open-contribution, modular, persistent, multi-level MMORPG that can also function as a cinematic production studio. Players and creators can contribute functioning worlds, maps, mechanics, machines, quests, and performances. The world can retain what actually happened and carry consequences forward.

The important film-selection rule is that the movie's protagonist and winning group are **not predetermined by the screenplay**:

1. Players and groups actually play through the evolving world.
2. The genuine raid-winning group determines the eventual central player/party for that story. Credit the group's contribution, not only the last hit.
3. After the outcome, reconstruct the winning group's real journey backward to character creation: decisions, failures, allies, rivals, discoveries, crafted objects, equipment exchanges, places visited, and other players who affected the result.
4. The studio then turns that evidence into a directed cinematic story and presents the journey forward in time.
5. The resulting film or episodic cinematic series is not raw gameplay footage, a Twitch highlight compilation, or a fabricated story about a player who did not win.

Played-through characters may persist as NPCs with their actual appearance, equipment, skills, history, relationships, and provenance. Retired player characters may later become bosses. Community events and accomplishments may feed future official content, including a monthly map/content or raid-boss cycle. Preserve previous world history when new content is added.

### Event and replay evidence
A clip is not the world history by itself. Where the engine supports it, record opt-in creator clips and reactions alongside authoritative event IDs, timestamps, locations, character identity, object lineage, party/raid receipts, and world-state changes. Preserve the event and relevant state evidence before the eventual winner is known. The final movie can then be directed and rendered using the shared environments, characters, modular parts, animation rigs, cameras, and scene data.

Creator rights, opt-in, permissions, attribution, and the distinction between real event and dramatized adaptation are essential. A scripted reconstruction must not be relabelled a verbatim transcript or documentary proof.

## 4. The movie / Netflix-style series

The film and episodic series are not promotional footage pasted on top of a game. They are a creative production stream connected to the same lived world.

The source production concept is to **record what is actually happening, then turn that record into a scripted science-fiction series**. The raw source includes the maker's decisions, discoveries, conversations, AI misunderstandings, corrections, failed attempts, repairs, jokes, arguments, and working results. The cinematic adaptation may compress, dramatize, combine, stage, visualize, and give events rhythm, but historical source and dramatic invention must remain distinguishable.

The owner has used the Netflix wordplay deliberately: a **net of connected lives** and a continuing **stream of flicks**. Preserve that playful naming idea. A streaming/television outlet is an aspiration in the source objective, **not evidence of a Netflix contract, partnership, commission, or distribution agreement**.

The movie/series should be watchable as a complete cinematic work even by someone who does not know it came from a game. It should be professionally directed and edited: camera, performance, framing, story, music, and sound are part of the work. The game provides authoritative history and reusable world assets; filmmakers create the cinematic telling.

## 5. Cross-platform video MMORPG: the long-horizon design target

The target experience is a persistent, modular, player-created MMORPG that can be inhabited and influenced through different devices, interfaces, worlds, and forms of participation. The cross-platform intention is to share world identity and compatible data while allowing each platform and engine to implement its own rendering, input, physics, and UI.

### Owner-canon objectives already recorded
- A public, modular engine/repository and creator tools can allow players to contribute worlds, maps, mechanics, machines, quests, and performances.
- The world is persistent. Player history, characters, items, relationships, and meaningful consequences should not silently disappear between sessions or content cycles.
- Played-through characters can persist as NPCs and carry their real history forward.
- Community play can determine future official content and cinematic protagonists.
- Game environments, androids, modular parts, assembly processes, inventory concepts, avatars, animation rigs, cameras, and scene data can be reused in cinematic production.
- Godot, Unity, Roblox, and Unreal implementations remain separate implementations. Shared canon or a common data contract does not prove that cross-play or engine-to-engine save compatibility has been built.
- The long-term direction includes AR/VR/AI participation and a life-scale interactive layer. This is a design horizon, not a claim that current devices or AI agents already provide the complete experience.

### Cross-platform boundaries
1. **World identity:** use stable world, character, entity, item, event, and creator identifiers where the relevant system supports them.
2. **Game-local implementation:** each game owns local physics, input, camera, rendering, scene structure, and interaction rules.
3. **Portable state:** define explicit schemas and adapters for data that truly travels between games. Do not infer compatibility from similar item names or visual designs.
4. **Persistence:** version saves, preserve source state, and migrate reversibly. Do not silently reset, merge, or convert old saves.
5. **Cross-play:** claim cross-platform multiplayer only after platform-specific authentication, networking, permissions, synchronization, latency, security, and recovery tests have passed.
6. **Media provenance:** preserve event identity, character and item lineage, source clips, permissions, and adaptation status independently of the rendered movie.

### Existing universal standards reference
[GAME_STANDARDS.md](GAME_STANDARDS.md), [UNIVERSAL_GAME_SCHEMA.json](UNIVERSAL_GAME_SCHEMA.json), and [UNIVERSAL_GAME_EXAMPLE.json](UNIVERSAL_GAME_EXAMPLE.json) define an October 30 shared-data foundation for compatible games, including portable items, backpack entries, spawner templates, game-local spawn points, save/load, and transitions. The standards document explicitly states that its specification is **not** evidence every game has implemented the shared backpack or transition system. The Aqua Workshop local WorldSeed inventory ↔ universal shared backpack remains **UNRESOLVED / NOT VERIFIED**.

## 6. Maker principle: show the line that supplies the world

Ryan's 26 November explanation is an owner-stated clarification of how world placement should be understood:

The world should not cut from town to dungeon, shop menu to chest open, or spawn point to monsters while hiding the supply mechanism. It should show the infrastructure and route between source and result. A chest's contents, a shop's stock, a drop, and a spawn are caused by a line of infrastructure that places and supplies them.

The recorded spawn-point rule is that the same unrepaired/broken infrastructure produces monsters; repairing that same infrastructure changes its function into a Player Spawner. The relevant GitHub recovery record explicitly labels this **historical / design record**, not verified live implementation: [Mesh / Four Lenses / Spawner Recovery](recovery/TO_WORLD_MESH_FOUR_LENSES_SPAWNER_RECOVERY_2026-11-26.md).

The four experiential lenses on the same mesh are **Fantasy / Science / Manufacturing / Virtual**. They are not four products or four separate worlds. Keep those lenses distinct from the four mechanism-governance layers:

- **Orbital:** celestial mechanics and trajectories.
- **Gate:** GERTECH pocket connections.
- **Workshop:** processing, fabrication, assembly, repair, and material accounting.
- **WorldSeed:** persistent spatial structure, entities, IDs, topology, and saved world state.

See [World Layers Addendum](canon/GERTECH_WORLD_LAYERS_ADDENDUM_2026-11-26.md) and [GERTECH Orbital Network Design](design/GERTECH_ORBITAL_NETWORK_DESIGN.md). The Orbital / Near-Dyson design lane remains DESIGN ONLY as labelled in its canonical design page.

## 8. Proposed build sequence (ASSISTANT-PROPOSED, pending Ryan's direction)

This is a planning synthesis of the existing owner objective and current source records. It is not a GO to start all phases at once, not a new canon hierarchy, and not permission to alter protected builds or film routing.

### Phase A — Complete the shared source map
- Read the current World Bible, controlled glossary, full name/identity registers, dialogue bank, complete chat exports, current film router, game standards, and active build registers.
- Link source originals and versions. Make provenance visible: OWNER-STATED, OWNER-SUPPLIED, OWNER-APPROVED, ASSISTANT-PROPOSED, INFERENCE, UNVERIFIED, SUPERSEDED.
- Preserve exact Ryan language in source context. Do not promote a summary or transcript reconstruction as a verbatim chat export.
- Identify access gaps, duplicate exports, missing originals, and contradictions for Ryan. Do not rewrite the source documents to make the index look tidy.

### Phase B — Verify one existing playable foundation
- Start with the actual current source project and intended engine environment, not a new rewrite.
- Import and launch the current build; record exact engine version, errors, scene, and runtime evidence.
- Complete the playable loop and verify real inputs, outputs, receipts, save/load, reset and recovery.
- Preserve a known-good project/save copy. No feature expansion should obscure whether the current baseline works.

### Phase C — Define shared world data before cross-engine claims
- Identify which identities and events must persist across game perspectives.
- Version stable IDs for worlds, players/characters, items, assets, events, and creator provenance.
- Define a portable data contract for shared data, while leaving platform-local behavior to each game.
- Specify explicit adapters and reversible migrations. Do not merge inventories, schemas, or save files by assumption.
- Test round-trip transitions between two small controlled test experiences before broadening.

### Phase D — Build the creator contribution loop
- Establish creator permissions, safe contribution review, source ownership, attribution, versioning, and rollback.
- Let players/creators contribute a contained world/map/mechanic/quest/asset package through a documented interface.
- Verify that a contribution can be loaded, tested, reviewed, promoted, and later retired without corrupting the persistent world.
- Establish the owner-defined monthly content/raid cycle only when its event history, contribution and moderation rules are explicit.

### Phase E — Connect play history to cinematic production
- Record authoritative game events and state evidence as play occurs, with creator permission and exact provenance.
- Preserve opt-in clips and original assets separately from extracted dialogue, editorial summaries, and adapted screenplays.
- Select the film protagonist/group only after the actual game outcome, then reconstruct their lived path and adapt it into a directed story.
- Reuse the game world's compatible assets and animation/camera data where practical, while maintaining cinematic craft and separate film approvals.
- Keep the existing film router, screenplay, cast, render queue and approved visual references controlling. A development plan cannot silently reorder them.

### Phase F — Expand platforms and participation one proof at a time
- Select a contained feature and implement it separately in each intended engine/platform.
- Test networking, identity, state, controls, device performance, accessibility, moderation, security and save/recovery per platform.
- Add AR/VR and more autonomous AI capabilities only as current platform capabilities and owner-authorized scopes support them.
- Expand from local tests to connected worlds only when explicit adapters and end-to-end evidence show the connection works.

### Completion evidence
Every milestone should report: source/version, owner direction, implementation path, test environment, test steps, observed result, evidence, limitations, and owner acceptance state. Distinguish **DESIGN ONLY**, **SOURCE PRESENT**, **RUNTIME TESTED**, **OWNER ACCEPTED**, and **PUBLISHED**.
