# PRIORITY ZERO — READY MAKER ONE: THE LIVING WORLD BECOMES CINEMA
**Owner canon, 2026-11-17. Highest-level objective for new TO WORLD / GERTECH / Ready Maker One projects and cross-engine handoffs.**

> The game determines who wins. The history reveals how. The studio discovers and makes the movie inside it.
>
> The journey is the lived experience; the fable is what it comes to mean; the story is the cinematic telling; time and framing make the picture convey it.

## The persistent objective

Build an open-contribution, modular, persistent, multi-level, holographic MMORPG that the owner actually creates, governs, inhabits, plays and stars in, and that doubles as a cinematic production studio. Community play becomes real in-world history. The most compelling results can become professionally directed motion pictures or an episodic cinematic series—not raw gameplay footage and not a Twitch highlight compilation. The audience can enjoy the movie without even knowing it came from a game. Never sacrifice this long-term purpose for short-term feature simplification.

**Identity:** **READY MAKER ONE** is the owner's established fan-fiction sequel concept to *Ready Player One*: *The LEGO Movie* meets *Wreck-It Ralph* for real, with holograms and levels. The owner is an in-world player and difficult-to-find boss. Finding him may yield unusual rewards. Favoritism and nepotism are intentional fictional social dynamics. Preserve named concepts and their boundaries: a verified connection to TO WORLD / GERTECH is permitted, but do not silently collapse separately established stories into one.

## The players make the world

- The public GitHub repository and modular engine are designed to let real players/creators contribute functioning worlds, maps, mechanics, machines, quests and performances. This expands the owner's *StarCraft* map-maker dream: community creations can be officially promoted, including a recurring map/content-of-the-month.
- The owner's and friends' actual gameplay helps produce the next month's bosses and official content, rather than a fixed designer-only schedule.
- Played-through characters can persist as NPCs with their actual appearance, equipment, skills, provenance, relationships and history. Retired player characters may later be bosses. This evokes *Overlord* and *Solo Leveling* as inspirations, not mandates to copy their material.
- Preserve what really happened. A month adds world history; it does not simply erase the preceding month.

## The game selects the protagonist AFTER the raid

1. Allow the real world, players and groups to evolve. Do not predetermine a movie hero, winner or cast.
2. Establish who actually reaches the raid boss and which group gets the kill. Honor group contributions, not just the last hit.
3. Starting from that outcome, trace the actual winning player(s) and group backward to character creation: decisions, setbacks, friends, rivalries, discoveries, tools, crafted-item origin, exchanged equipment, changed locations and other players who affected the result.
4. Extract the lived journey and shape its meaning into a fable. Write and produce the film to **present that history forward in time**, with deliberate direction, framing, camera work, performance, editing, music and sound.
5. The game establishes the facts; creative filmmakers establish the cinematic telling. Do not substitute a made-up winner, erase allies, or pretend a clip montage is a finished motion picture.

## Twitch-style clip discovery and provenance

Existing creator streams and AI-assisted clip/highlight mechanics can help identify compelling moments. Do **not** claim a need to invent generic AI clip detection from nothing. Link opt-in clips and reactions to authoritative in-game event IDs, timestamps, locations, character identity, object lineage, party/raid receipts and world-state changes. Preserve enough structured events, state checkpoints, assets and selected replay information to reconstruct consequential scenes after the raid, before anyone knows the eventual victor. Clips are reference evidence; the final movie is cinematically directed and rendered within the reusable world. Obtain permissions and give appropriate creator credit.

## The same world is playable and filmable

- Reuse environments, androids, modular parts, assembly processes, inventory, avatars, animation rigs, cameras and scene data across actual gameplay and cinematic production.
- Enable skilled streamers, builders, performers, writers and animators to collaborate on a distributed studio. A streaming/television outlet is an aspiration, not a signed distribution deal.
- Keep game rules, world data, event history, film adaptation and creator rights as distinguishable layers. Avoid a design where a video capture is the only historical record.
- Keep Godot, Unity, Roblox and Unreal implementations separate while sharing canon and validating capabilities through real tests. No engine is chosen by wishful description.

## Rule for EVERY new related project or handoff

Before implementation, write a short **Priority Zero alignment statement** describing which part of the cycle this project advances: (1) modular creator tools, (2) persistent characters/world, (3) player-generated monthly content/raid outcomes, (4) event and clip provenance, (5) game-to-cinematic asset reuse, or (6) reverse-engineering the winning journey into a directed film.

**Preservation law:** *An addition should preserve everything that exists and add new capabilities. It should never silently replace an established system with a simpler description of it.*

This is a governing **long-term objective**, not a new GO to alter current film routing, dialogue, approved imagery, shot timings, cast locks, game code or the owner's selected next task. Owner-stated corrections override assistant inferences. Preserve original source passages, existing successful versions and linked records.
